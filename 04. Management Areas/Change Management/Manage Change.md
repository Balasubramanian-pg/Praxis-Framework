# Manage Change Competency: Driving Enterprise Analytics Adoption

> [!SUMMARY] Overview
> Demonstrates the capability to lead the cultural, behavioural, and operational transitions required to move business stakeholders off unversioned shadow spreadsheets and embed certified Lakehouse semantic models into daily decision-making routines.

## Purpose
The **Manage Change** competency certifies that a project manager, business change manager (BCM), or data product owner can systematically plan, lead, and sustain the human and organizational transformations essential for analytics project success.

In enterprise Data & Business Intelligence initiatives, technical deployment is merely half the battle. A project manager who cannot manage change delivers "shelfware"—expensive lakehouses and polished dashboards that business teams ignore in favor of their familiar, ungoverned desktop spreadsheets.

```
                    The Modern Analytics Change Architecture
                    
[Current State: Shadow IT]      [Transition State: The Governed Bridge]      [Future State: Data-Driven]
- Ungoverned Excel extracts  ──► - Departmental Data Champions        ──► - Certified Semantic Layer
- Conflicting revenue KPIs       - "Analyze in Excel" Live Bridges         - Automated Morning Refreshes
- Weekly manual data scraping    - Weekly Data Office Hours & Training     - Audited Decision Velocity
```

## Performance Criteria

An individual certified in this competency demonstrates the following practical capabilities:

1. **Assess Analytical Readiness & Cultural Barriers**:
   - Evaluate the host department's baseline data literacy and cultural maturity (e.g., assessing whether decisions are driven by data evidence or political HIPPO—*Highest Paid Person's Opinion*).
   - Identify resistance drivers: fear of metric exposure (transparency anxiety), fear of central IT bottlenecking, or lack of confidence in interpreting statistical trends.

2. **Formulate the Data Adoption & Training Strategy**:
   - Design role-tiered enablement pathways:
     * **Executive Consumers**: 15-minute briefings on KPI card interpretation, mobile navigation, and anomaly drill-downs.
     * **Department Analysts**: Deep-dive workshops on Power BI / Tableau self-service slicing, DAX measures, and dbt semantic layer queries.
     * **Data Champions**: Advanced training on data contract specifications, feedback logging, and tier-1 peer support.

3. **Deploy Governed Bridges to Mitigate Spreadsheet Resistance**:
   - Avoid abruptly banning spreadsheets, which drives users into covert shadow databases.
   - Configure XMLA endpoints and "Analyze in Excel" live connections, allowing analysts to query certified Gold models from inside familiar Excel pivot tables without maintaining local data extracts.

4. **Monitor Adoption Telemetry & Execute Sunsetting**:
   - Audit usage telemetry (Power BI Activity Logs, Snowflake query histories) to track Daily Active Users (DAU) and Weekly Active Users (WAU).
   - Partner with executive sponsors to systematically decommission legacy CSV email distribution and revoke read-replica database credentials once user proficiency is validated.

## Knowledge & Competence Criteria

To achieve proficiency, the practitioner must master:

- **The Formula for Change in Analytics**: Applying $C = (A \times B \times D) > X$ to quantify user dissatisfaction ($A$), the desirability of a single source of truth ($B$), practical MVP steps ($D$), and perceived transition friction ($X$).
- **Data Literacy Frameworks**: Teaching non-technical stakeholders to distinguish between correlation vs. causation, understand confidence intervals, and interpret metric lineage.
- **Change Resistance Psychology**: Navigating the "Valley of Despair" when business users temporarily experience lower productivity during the learning curve of a new analytics tool.

## Flashcard Q&A

- **Q:** Why do business users frequently resist adopting enterprise BI dashboards in favor of Excel?
    - **A:** Spreadsheets provide autonomy, instant flexibility, and complete formatting control without waiting for central IT approval. Overcoming this requires providing governed live connections ("Analyze in Excel") rather than forcing an abrupt, rigid tool migration.

- **Q:** What is the role of a "Departmental Data Champion" in change management?
    - **A:** An analytical super-user embedded within a business unit (Finance, Sales, Ops) who understands domain context, champions the certified semantic model, and provides peer-level coaching to drive local adoption.

- **Q:** How does a project manager use telemetry to verify that organizational change has taken root?
    - **A:** By monitoring usage logs (MAU/WAU ratios, query volume, report view frequencies). Sustained daily usage across departments proves the new platform has become the standard operational way of working.
